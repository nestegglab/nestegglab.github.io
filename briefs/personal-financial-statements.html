Personal financial statements and the family

What the US accounting standard for personal financial statements does, where it is under pressure, how other fields measure household wealth, and what that means for statements built for families.

Richard Berk (ORCID 0009-0004-5344-4614) · Nest Egg Lab · November 2026

Key points

  1. There is already a standard. ASC 274 sets US accounting rules for personal financial statements. It values assets at what they could sell for today, deducts the tax that would be due on the gains, and allows statements for a person, a married couple or a family.
  2. It is old and largely unexamined. It came from an AICPA statement of 1982 and was carried into the FASB’s Codification without fresh deliberation. In March 2026 the FASB voted not to revisit it.
  3. It was built mainly for outside readers, above all lenders. Its core question is what a person is worth on one date. It handles joint ownership by counting only each person’s beneficial interest, but it has no way to show who stands to inherit, who controls an asset, or what it is meant for.
  4. Other fields have faced the same choice of unit. National statistics count the household, assuming its members share. Studies with person-level data find large wealth gaps inside couples, which a household total hides.
  5. A family statement can build on ASC 274 rather than replace it. Keep its measurement rules, add the questions a family needs answered, and combine statements by agreement rather than by adding them up.

1. The standard and where it came from

ASC 274 is the topic of the FASB’s Accounting Standards Codification that governs personal financial statements in the United States. Its stated focus is a person’s assets and liabilities, and it presents them at estimated current value because that is what its users are said to need: lenders assessing collateral, people doing estate, gift and tax planning, and candidates filing public disclosures.

  • 1979An AICPA issues paper on personal financial statements, later superseded by the AICPA guide.
  • October 1982AICPA Statement of Position 82-1, Accounting and Financial Reporting for Personal Financial Statements, requires estimated current values. Effective for statements dated June 30, 1983 and after.
  • 1983The AICPA Personal Financial Statements Guide sets out how to compile, review and audit such statements. It is updated through 2008.
  • 2008–09The FASB builds its Codification and carries SOP 82-1 in as Topic 274. The Board did not deliberate the content; it codified it.
  • August 2024Daniel Tinkelman (Brooklyn College) asks the FASB to reconsider Topic 274, citing ambiguities exposed in the New York civil fraud case against Donald Trump.
  • 2024–25The FASB’s agenda consultation records requests to move to fair value and improve disclosures. Most respondents rank the topic low priority.
  • March 25, 2026The FASB votes not to take up a project. Board members call the current approach more practical and note the guidance came from the AICPA.

2. How it measures

The rules below are from SOP 82-1 as reproduced in the AICPA guide; ASC 274 carries them forward. Paragraph numbers are the SOP’s.

Whose statement

Statements may be prepared for an individual, a husband and wife, or a family (¶5). Where a person is one of several joint owners, the statement includes only that person’s interest as a beneficial owner, determined under state property law; joint tenancy and community property may need legal advice to sort out (¶9). A closely held business is shown as one net investment, never mixed with personal items (¶10).

What each item is worth

What must be disclosed

Among other things (¶31): who the statement covers; that values are estimates; the methods used and any change in them; the nature of jointly owned assets; concentrated holdings; details of closely held businesses; the face amount of life insurance; future rights that did not qualify as assets, such as a pension that depends on living to collect it; and the assumptions behind the tax estimate.

The future-interest test matters for families. Many of the things a family most depends on, such as a pension for life or an interest in a trust that pays at a trustee’s discretion, fail it. They are disclosed in a note, not counted.

3. How it is used in practice

  • Lenders and their formsBanks and the SBA often use their own personal financial statement forms rather than ASC 274 statements. The SBA’s Form 413 asks each married applicant’s spouse to complete a separate form unless legally separated: in practice a person-level unit.
  • Accountants’ engagementsA CPA can prepare, compile, review or audit a personal statement under the AICPA’s SSARS and auditing standards. Statements prepared only for a written personal financial plan have long had an exemption, because they often omit disclosures and depart from GAAP.
  • Common errorsPeer reviewers report preparers measuring personal statements as if they were business statements and failing to disclose valuation methods. The AICPA guide lists leaving out the tax provision as a likely misstatement.

No source found for this brief measures how many personal financial statements are prepared each year or by whom. The ones that become public, such as those in the Trump case, are not typical.

Documented; prevalence unknown

4. Where the standard is under pressure

The New York case turned a quiet corner of accounting into a trial exhibit. The defendant testified that, because GAAP was so flexible, he considered his own statements “worthless”. Tinkelman’s letter to the FASB distils four problems.

“Could” or “would”?Estimated current value is defined nowhere else

The term appears nowhere else in the Codification and has no guidance outside Topic 274. The parties disputed whether the amount an item “could” be exchanged for allows “as if” values, assuming restrictions lifted or land developed, or must be what a buyer and seller would agree for the asset as it stands. The proposed fix is to use fair value as defined in ASC 820.

Methods or result?Lists of valuation methods read as permission

The implementation guidance lists methods for businesses and real estate, from earnings multiples to assessed values to adjusted cost. The defence argued these lists allow any listed method even when results differ widely from any price a buyer would pay. One defence expert’s range for a single valuation ran from $18 million to $800–900 million depending on method. The proposed fix: methods only approximate the value and cannot be used when the result is clearly outside the plausible range.

“Net worth” without the taxThe provision that is easiest to leave out

Net worth is defined after estimated income tax on unrealised gains. The statements in the case omitted that provision, and the compilation reports flagged the departure without measuring it; the letter estimates the omitted tax in the hundreds of millions. The proposed fix: forbid the term “net worth” unless the tax is deducted.

Changes in method, one year at a timeDisclosure that only bites with comparatives

Changes in valuation method must be disclosed. The defence argued this applies only when comparative statements are presented, and all the statements in the case were single-year. The proposed fix: require disclosure whatever the format.

The FASB declined to act. Its staff questioned whether the problems were pervasive, which a project requires, and one member called the current approach more practical and cost-effective. At least one large firm told the FASB it did not support prioritising the topic. The ambiguities therefore stand, and anyone building on ASC 274 has to resolve them for themselves.

5. How other fields approach the same problem

Accounting is not the only field that measures what households own. Each of the approaches below chooses a unit, a value and a view of how members share, and each choice reveals something the others hide.

Household statistics: the household as one unitOECD guidelines and the Survey of Consumer Finances

The OECD’s 2013 guidelines were the first internationally agreed standard for micro statistics on household wealth, and the OECD’s wealth database built on them takes the household as its unit of observation and analysis. National statistics offices that follow them note that although assets may be owned individually, the benefit is shared at least to some extent among members.

The Federal Reserve’s Survey of Consumer Finances uses a narrower unit, the “primary economic unit”: the economically dominant individual or couple and those who depend on them. Financially independent adults in the same home are left out.

Documented
Person-level data: what household totals hideWealth gaps inside couples

Most wealth research rests on the assumption that resources are pooled and shared equally inside the household, largely because person-level data are rare. The German Socio-Economic Panel is an exception. Using it, Sierminska, Frick and Grabka found a gender wealth gap of about €50,000 between married partners, driven mostly by each person’s own income and work history. Grabka, Marcus and Sierminska later found men in couples held on average €33,000 more net worth than their partners.

For a family statement this is the strongest argument for the person as the unit. Adding a couple’s or a family’s wealth together erases exactly the differences that shape who can decide what.

Documented
Tax valuation: pricing interests that ASC 274 leaves outSection 7520 actuarial tables

For estate and gift tax, US law already values the interests a family statement struggles with. Annuities, life estates, remainders and reversions are valued as a present value using standard section 7520 actuarial factors, which combine a published interest rate with mortality tables. The IRS publishes the tables free. They do not apply to qualified retirement plans.

This offers a ready, checkable method for interests that fail ASC 274’s future-interest test, such as a parent’s life interest in a home with the remainder to children. It is a tax convention, not a market price, and should be labelled as such.

Documented
Household accounting and the meaning of moneyAccounting at home, and earmarking

A small accounting literature studies accounting in the home. Walker and Llewellyn argued that the field had ignored the household as unworthy of study, though accounting there has social effects as large as in public institutions. Northcott and Doolin found that simple home budgets and records give people a sense of control and help them justify choices, with little difference between accountants and lay people. Historical work on Australian households found accounting used, among other things, to restrain women’s spending. Pahl’s studies of couples read their money arrangements as systems of accountability.

In sociology, Zelizer showed that families earmark money for particular people and purposes rather than treating every dollar alike, and the US Financial Diaries later documented how often low- and moderate-income households earmark. A statement that records only amounts misses what families most argue about: whose money it is, and what it is for.

Documented; mostly qualitative

6. What this means for family financial statements

The lab’s draft family financial statements start from ASC 274 and add what a family needs. This review suggests six design choices, in order of priority.

Gap or findingDesign choice
ASC 274’s measurement rules are workable and familiar to accountantsAdopt them as they stand: estimated current value, the tax provision, the future-interest test and its disclosures. Build a family layer on top, not a rival standard.
“Could” versus “would” is unresolvedState the answer: the price a buyer would pay for the asset as it stands, with no “as if” values. For statements prepared by AI assistants, this matters more, not less.
“Net worth” is reported without taxUse the term only when the tax provision is included; otherwise say “assets less debts, before tax”.
Household totals hide gaps between membersMake the person the unit. Combine only by agreement, side by side.
ASC 274 records beneficial ownership today, but not who will benefit later, who decides, or what an asset is forKeep ASC 274’s beneficial interest as the measured share, and add three tags: expected beneficiaries, decision rights, and purpose. Ground the purpose tag in the research on earmarking.
Life and trust interests fail the future-interest testKeep them out of the totals as ASC 274 does, but allow an optional value from the section 7520 tables, clearly labelled as a tax convention.

The concepts paper now applies these choices. These are proposals for review, not findings. The next step is to read the codified text of Topic 274 itself, since this brief relies on SOP 82-1 as reproduced by the AICPA and on secondary summaries.
