How family fortunes last

Our research program studies how large fortunes persist across generations, what has historically limited them, and the legal and financial structures that carry them today.

The question

How do family fortunes pass from one generation to the next, what slows them down, and how much of this can we actually see?

Most research on wealth concentration measures who is wealthy at a point in time. We study the machinery of persistence: the trusts, family offices and tax rules that carry wealth across generations, and the features of those structures that determine whether a fortune grows, holds steady or wears down over time.

The brakes

Historically, several forces have limited how long a fortune keeps compounding. We call them brakes. Our work asks how strong each one is, how it has changed, and which modern structures are designed around it.

  • Payouts to heirsIncome paid out of a trust and spent is no longer compounding.
  • Tax at each generationEstate and generation-skipping taxes apply when wealth passes to the next generation, unless it is sheltered.
  • Trusts that endTraditional trusts ended after a generation or two and divided their assets among heirs.
  • Taxes and costs along the wayIncome tax inside the trust, trustee fees and administration.
  • Division among heirsEach generation splits a fortune among more people.

Many of these brakes are matters of law and practice, and they have changed considerably since the 1980s. Some modern structures are also largely invisible in official statistics, which is itself part of what we study.

Research areas

Active

Trusts

What dynasty trusts do to wealth concentration, and what the historical record of American trusts shows: their size, payouts, costs and funding since the 1950s.

Hidden Dynasties (model, working paper). A companion study measuring the trust sector from tax and financial data is in progress.

Planned

Family offices

The organizations that manage large family fortunes: who runs them, what they cost, how they make decisions about trusts and payouts, and how visible they are in public data.

Scoped

Tax basis and inheritance

How the treatment of capital gains at death shapes what families hold, sell and pass on, and how that interacts with trusts.

Ongoing

Uses and limits

Trusts and family offices serve real purposes: caring for minors and dependents, managing money for those who cannot, keeping family businesses intact. We ask which of those purposes depend on permanence and limited disclosure, and which do not.

How we work

We build models and assemble data side by side. Models show what a structure would do under stated assumptions; data show what has actually happened. Each project records its predictions before results are run, reports its misses, and publishes its code, data sources and decision log. We study how the rules work and what they do, and leave policy choices to others.
